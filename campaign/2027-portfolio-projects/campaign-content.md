**Caption**

A fixed project price should be clear.

For these ten selected 2027 commissions, the price is agreed around the original design direction, body placement and coverage.

If that agreed tattoo needs additional tattooing time to reach the intended finish, that time is included. You won't be charged extra simply because the original scope takes longer than the minimum estimate.

If the brief changes — for example, extra coverage, a different body area or a replacement concept — we discuss and agree any variation before proceeding.

The point is clarity: one agreed commission, one agreed price, and a shared understanding of the work.

## 9. DM response — price question

The selected project prices are £450 for a quarter sleeve, £900 for a half sleeve and £1,800 for a full sleeve.

Those are fixed prices for the agreed original scope. If additional tattooing time is needed to complete that scope, it is included. Additional coverage or substantial changes to the agreed brief would be discussed and quoted separately.

The full details are here: https://danhughestattoos.co.uk/2027-portfolio-projects/

## 10. DM response — “How many sessions?”

The minimum estimates are 1.5 days for a quarter sleeve, 3 days for a half sleeve and 6 days for a full sleeve. These are planning estimates rather than a limit. The selected fixed price covers the tattooing time needed to complete the original agreed scope.

## 12. Creative asset checklist

For each published asset, use:
- Genuine Daniel Hughes tattoo photography, with recorded permission.
- Correct crop: 4:5 feed/carousel, 9:16 Reel/Story.
- Minimal overlay copy; keep the tattoo itself visible.
- Consistent black, warm ivory and muted bronze campaign treatment.
- Accurate colour and skin tone; do not digitally alter tattoo quality.
- Alt text for accessibility.
- Campaign URL with UTM parameters for paid and organic sources.

**Not yet supplied:** final healed tattoo photographs, portrait/headshot, process clips and approved logo/wordmark files. Do not publish placeholder concepts as completed work.
