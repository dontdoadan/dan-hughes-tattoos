01 / Just finished

First, your dressing.

Your tattoo is a fresh wound. Keep it protected on the way home and only handle it with freshly washed hands.

Before you remove anything: use the removal and replacement timings given at your appointment. If you’re unsure which dressing you have or when to remove it, contact me.
Cling film / standard dressing

Follow your appointment instructions.

Remove or replace the covering at the time you were told. Don’t keep rewrapping it without advice. Once removed, follow the gentle cleaning routine below.

Adhesive film / “second skin”

Follow the instructions for that film.

Wear time and removal vary by product. Don’t apply cream underneath it or puncture it. If it leaks, loses its seal over the tattoo or irritates your skin, follow the product’s removal guidance and contact me.

02 / Once the dressing is off

Keep the routine gentle.

Repeat the care discussed at your appointment. Keep the area clean, and avoid unnecessary touching between washes.

01.

Wash gently

Wash and dry your hands first. Gently clean the tattoo using lukewarm water and a mild, fragrance-free soap. Use clean fingertips, without scrubbing or exfoliating.

02.

Pat dry

Rinse away the soap, then gently pat dry with a clean disposable paper towel. Don’t rub, and don’t share a towel with anyone else.

03.

Apply a little aftercare

If recommended at your appointment, apply a thin layer of the advised aftercare product to dry skin. Avoid a thick coating. Stop using a product if it irritates your skin and seek advice.

03 / Give it room to heal

A few things to skip.

Protect the area until it has healed. Surface healing varies, so use the condition of your skin and the advice you’ve been given rather than a fixed date.

↗

Soaking & swimming

Avoid baths, pools, hot tubs and the sea while healing. Keep showers brief and avoid a strong spray on the tattoo.

☼

Sun & sunbeds

Keep a fresh tattoo out of direct sunlight. Once fully healed, protect it with broad-spectrum SPF 30+ and clothing.

×

Picking & scratching

Leave scabs and flakes alone. Don’t shave or exfoliate the area while it heals.

≈

Rubbing & heavy sweating

Choose clean, loose clothing. Avoid exercise that rubs, stretches or irritates the tattoo, and keep it off shared gym surfaces.

04 / Know what to look for

Healing, and when to get help.

Some changes are expected. Symptoms should gradually settle, not become more intense. Redness can be less visible on darker skin; also watch for heat, swelling and increasing pain.

Can be part of normal healing

Settling down over time

  • Mild tenderness, redness or swelling soon after your session.
  • A little clear fluid or ink-coloured fluid early on.
  • Itching, flaking or light scabbing as the surface heals.

The key is improvement. Don’t pick at peeling skin.

Get medical advice promptly

Getting worse, rather than better

  • Spreading redness, increasing heat or swelling.
  • Worsening pain or severe throbbing.
  • Pus, an unpleasant smell or open sores.
  • Fever, chills or feeling unwell.

Contact your GP or NHS 111 promptly if you suspect an infection. Don’t wait for a reply from the studio.

Get help from NHS 111 ↗

Emergency: call 999 for difficulty breathing, swelling of the mouth or throat, or collapse.

05 / Good to know

A few common questions.

How long will my tattoo take to heal?

Healing varies with the size, placement and your skin. The surface may settle over a few weeks, while deeper healing takes longer. Don’t assume it is ready for swimming or friction just because it looks better. Ask if you’re unsure.

What should I put on my tattoo?

Use the product recommended at your appointment, sparingly. Avoid experimenting with scented products or home remedies. Don’t use antibiotic cream unless a clinician advises it.

How should I sleep with a fresh tattoo?

Use clean bedding and avoid pressure or rubbing on the tattoo where possible. Follow your dressing instructions overnight. If fabric sticks, gently moisten it with clean lukewarm water to release it; don’t pull it off dry.

Can I send a photo if I’m unsure?

Yes. Reply to your follow-up email with a clear photo in natural light, when the tattoo was done, and what has changed. A photo can help with aftercare questions, but it cannot rule out infection. For warning signs, contact a healthcare professional promptly.
