# Content Production Plan

## Objective

Turn each of the ten commissions into a documented portfolio case study, while keeping client permission separate from the booking and application process.

## Capture stages

### 1. Concept and brief
- Capture the agreed project brief and initial visual references.
- Record the chosen project direction and why it suits the client.
- Do not publish private client references without permission.

### 2. Design
- Photograph or record the design process, sketches and composition development.
- Capture the final design/stencil only after the client has approved it.
- Keep client-identifying details out of public content unless separately authorised.

### 3. Tattooing progress
- Capture wide, medium and close-up progress images.
- Record session number and date.
- Use consistent lighting and camera distance where possible.
- Avoid interrupting the tattoo process for content.

### 4. Completion
- Photograph the finished tattoo in neutral, consistent lighting.
- Capture full placement and detail crops.
- Record the completion date and actual tattooing hours.
- Do not digitally alter skin tone, texture or tattoo quality in a misleading way.

### 5. Healed result
- Arrange a healed follow-up where practical.
- Capture the same angles as the completion photos.
- Record the approximate healing interval.
- Create a before/after comparison only where both images are accurate and permissioned.

## Recommended deliverables per project

| Asset | Target |
|---|---:|
| Concept/design images | 3–5 |
| Process clips | 3–5 |
| Progress photographs | 5–10 |
| Finished photographs | 5–8 |
| Healed photographs | 5–8 |
| Reel | 1–2 |
| Carousel | 1 |
| Story sequences | 2–3 |
| Portfolio case study | 1 |

These are targets, not a reason to compromise the client experience.

## Shot list

- Full tattoo in natural body position.
- Full tattoo with the limb/body relaxed.
- Detail crops showing texture, contrast and transitions.
- Placement view showing how the design fits the body.
- Stencil or design-to-skin moment, if appropriate.
- Short process clips: setup, drawing, machine work, wipe/reveal.
- Finished reveal, with the client only if separately permitted.
- Healed comparison using consistent framing.

## Permission workflow

1. Before filming or photographing, explain what will be captured.
2. Use a separate written permission form; the booking agreement alone is not permission.
3. Record the allowed channels for each asset.
4. Record whether the client's face/name may appear.
5. Store the permission status against the asset in the campaign content register.
6. Do not publish assets marked pending, declined or withdrawn.
7. If permission is withdrawn, stop future use and remove content where reasonably practicable and legally required.

## Publishing formats

- Instagram feed/carousel: 4:5 portrait.
- Reels and Stories: 9:16 vertical.
- Website portfolio: landscape and portrait crops as appropriate.
- Keep the tattoo itself prominent; use restrained typography and minimal overlays.
- Use the campaign's black, warm ivory and muted bronze design treatment.
- Add useful alt text and descriptive captions.

## Content quality rules

- Never present a concept sketch or generated illustration as a completed tattoo.
- Never imply a tattoo is healed if it is fresh.
- Do not use filters that misrepresent colour, skin or tattoo detail.
- Do not publish client identity, face, voice or private references without explicit permission.
- Do not make medical claims about healing or tattoo safety.
